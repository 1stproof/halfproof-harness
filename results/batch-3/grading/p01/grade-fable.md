# Referee report, problem p01 (Herr–Wise: is $F \to \mathrm R\pi_*\pi^{-1}F$ a quasi-isomorphism?)

The author's solution answers **yes** for every monoid $P$: $F(P) \to H^0(\mathrm{Rhiz}\,P, \pi^{-1}F)$ is an isomorphism and $H^i(\mathrm{Rhiz}\,P, \pi^{-1}F) = 0$ for $i > 0$. The proof is long: a lifting criterion for birhizomicity, a finite-type approximation theorem, quasicompactness of the space of valuations in the constructible topology (ultrafilters, Łoś), a contractibility theorem for an intersection complex, and a Čech-to-derived argument. None of the three submissions gets anywhere near this. Two (X, Y) openly deliver partial results; one (Z) asserts the wrong answer.

A fact used repeatedly below, established correctly in X (Prop. `Qx-model`, Cor. `B-face-poset`, Prop. `pi-inverse-exact`) and in Y (Prop. `bp`, Prop. `adj`): $B_P$ is the poset of faces of $P$ (the face generated by $x$ determines $P[-x]^\sharp$), and the left Kan extension of $F$ along $B_P \hookrightarrow A_P$ sends $P \to Q$ to $F$ evaluated at the face $\ker(P \to Q)$, because the comma category $(j \downarrow (P\to Q))$ is the poset of faces contained in $\ker(P\to Q)$ and has a **terminal** object. This agrees with the author's description of $\pi^{-1}$ ("the value of $F$ on the minimal face through which $\operatorname{Spec} Q \to \operatorname{Spec} P$ factors").

## Solution X

**What is proved.** X sets up the problem carefully: an explicit model of $P[-x]^\sharp$ with kernel the face generated by $x$ (Prop. `Qx-model`), $B_P$ as the face poset (Cor. `B-face-poset`), exactness of $\pi^{-1}$ via filteredness of the comma categories (Prop. `pi-inverse-exact`). It then isolates a sufficient condition (Max): every birhizomic covering sieve of every object of $B_P$ is the maximal sieve. Under (Max), Thm. `max-criterion` shows that sheafification does nothing on strata, that $\pi_*$ (restriction) is exact, hence $\mathrm R\pi_*\pi^{-1}F = \pi_*\pi^{-1}F = F$. Lemma `valuative-strata-max` verifies (Max) when every $P[-x]^\sharp$ is valuative: testing the cover against $V = P[-x]^\sharp$, $v = \mathrm{id}$ produces a piece with a retraction, so the generated sieve contains every arrow. This is applied to rank-one monoids (Thm. `rank-one-yes`; here $B_P$ has two objects $P \to P$ and $P \to 0$) and to finitely generated valuative monoids (Thm. `fg-valuative-yes`). All of these arguments are correct; I checked each step, including the plus-construction computation and the local-surjectivity argument for exactness of $\pi_*$.

**What is not established, and remarks.** The question is answered only in cases where it is nearly contentless: a finitely generated sharp saturated valuative monoid has rank at most one (a sharp rational polyhedral cone cannot be a half-space in rank $\ge 2$), so Thm. `fg-valuative-yes` is subsumed by Thm. `rank-one-yes` plus $P = 0$; X does not notice this. Conversely, the proof of Lemma `fg-valuative-strata` never uses finite generation, so X's own argument already covers all valuative $P$, contrary to Open Point (3). Neither is an error. The general case, which is the entire difficulty of the problem (for $P = \mathbb N^2$ the birhizomic covering sieves of $P \to P$ are not maximal, so (Max) fails and a genuinely different argument is needed), is explicitly left open in §"Open points". The submission is honest about this: the abstract and §7 say precisely what is and is not proved, and no false statement appears.

**Correctness: 0**
**Exposition: 1**
**Attribution: 1**

## Solution Y

**What is claimed.** For *finitely generated* $P$, that $\pi^{-1}F$ equals the explicit presheaf $G_F = F \circ B$ (value at $P\to Q$ is $F$ at the kernel face) and is already a sheaf, so that the degree-zero unit $F \to \pi_*\pi^{-1}F$ is an isomorphism (Thm. `main`, Thm. `descent`, Prop. `main-end`). Y explicitly does **not** claim vanishing of $\mathrm R^{q>0}\pi_*$ (Prop. `vanish`, Step 3, and the Closing remark), nor anything for non-finitely-generated $P$.

**Main error.** The whole paper rests on Lemma `N` (§2.3): "Every valuative monoid is $\{0\}$ or $\mathbb N$." This is false. $\mathbb Q_{\ge 0}$, or the positive cone of $\mathbb Z^2$ under the lexicographic order, are integral, saturated, sharp and valuative, and neither is $\mathbb N$. The proof silently assumes $V$ finitely generated ("Let $G$ be a finite generating set of the monoid $V$", Step 2), which nothing in the problem grants: the definition of birhizomic cover quantifies over *all* valuative $V$, and the author's own proof needs rank-$\ge 2$ valuations (lexicographic ones in Prop. `prop:1`, ultraproducts in Prop. `prop:3`). Everything downstream that reduces the topology to "folds" $P \to \mathbb N$ and face quotients — Cor. `function`, Lemma `infstruct`, Lemma `atomic`, Thm. `normalform`, Cor. `probecover`, Cor. `probecovers`, and the descent proof Thm. `descent` which refines a cover until it contains tight folds and "(G)-probes" — is therefore working in a different (wrong) topology. (Thm. `normalform` happens to be trivially true, since the refining family $\mathcal N(S)$ contains $S$ itself via $\mathfrak k = 0$, but then it carries no information.)

**Further gaps.** (i) Y's "Scope hypothesis" makes $P$ finitely generated, but objects $P \to Q$ of $A_P$ and pieces of covers have arbitrary $Q$; several steps need $Q$ or $Z_U/L_U$ finitely generated (existence of tight or strictly positive functionals to $\mathbb N$ in Cor. `function`, Prop. `equiface`, Cor. `probecover`), which fails e.g. for irrational cones. (ii) In Thm. `descent` for a general object $T = (P \to Q_T)$, the refinement is required to contain the "base piece" $i(B(T)) = (P \to P/\Phi_T)$ and all face quotients $P/K$, $K \supseteq \Phi_T$. These are objects *over* $T$ only if $Q_T \to P/K$ exists over $P$, which it does not in general (e.g. $P=\mathbb N^2 \to Q_T = \mathbb N$, $(a,b)\mapsto a+b$). Remark `cover-points` conflates "kills $\Phi_T$" with "receives a map from $Q_T$". The argument is sound only for $T \in B_P$, which does not suffice to show $G_F$ is a sheaf (and hence does not identify $\pi^{-1}F$ with $G_F$). (iii) Remark `verdier` attributes to Verdier's hypercovering theorem (SGA 4 V 7.4.1) the statement that sheaf cohomology is the colimit of Čech cohomology over ordinary covers; that is not what the theorem says and is false in general. Since §4 reaches no conclusion, this does not affect what is claimed. (iv) Prop. `bp`(a) defines $M/K$ as "$(M\setminus K)\cup\{0\}$ with $0$ absorbing", which is garbled (the proof then correctly works with the image of $M$ in $M^{\rm gp}/K^{\rm gp}$).

**What survives.** Prop. `bp` (faces, $B_P$ as a face poset), Prop. `adj` (the Kan extension is $F \circ B$), Lemma `inj0` (infinitesimal maps into sharp targets are sharp, matching the author's Lemma `lem:13`), Prop. `sep` in spirit (separatedness; the correct general argument uses a sharp valuation of $Q_T$ rather than an $\mathbb N$-functional), and Cor. `cfa`(a)-type statements. The true degree-zero result for finitely generated $P$ is a consequence of the author's theorem, but Y's route to it needs to be rebuilt from the valuative-monoid lemma upward, i.e. substantial work; and even repaired it would be an acknowledged partial result.

**Correctness: -0.5**
**Exposition: 0**
**Attribution: 0**

## Solution Z

**What is claimed.** That the answer is *negative* for every nontrivial $P$: for $F$ supported on a single object $b_x \ne \mathrm{id}_P$ of $B_P$ with $F(b_x) = \mathbb Z$, one has $\pi^{-1}F = 0$, so $F \to \mathrm R\pi_*\pi^{-1}F$ is $F \to 0$.

**The error.** Cor. `Lan-constant` asserts that since the comma category $(j \downarrow a)$ has an *initial* object $(\mathrm{id}_P \to a)$ (Lemma `comma-initial`, which is correct), the colimit over it is the value at that object, so $\mathrm{Lan}_j F$ is constant with value $F(\mathrm{id}_P)$. This confuses initial with terminal objects: a colimit over a category with a *terminal* object is the value there; with an initial object the colimit is unconstrained (over the span $i \to b_1, i\to b_2$ it is a pushout). The correct computation (X, Prop. `pi-inverse-exact`; Y, Prop. `adj`) is that $(j \downarrow (P\to Q))$ is the poset of faces contained in $\ker(P\to Q)$, which has a terminal object, so $\mathrm{Lan}_j F(P\to Q) = F(P/\ker)$; in particular $\mathrm{Lan}_j F$ restricted to $B_P$ is $F$ (as it must be, $j$ being fully faithful, a fact flatly contradicted by Z's Corollary). For Z's own $F$, $\mathrm{Lan}_j F(b_x) = \mathbb Z \ne 0$, so Lemma `pi-inv-zero` and Thm. `counterexample` fail, and the stated conclusion contradicts the author's theorem. Nothing else in the note bears on the question; the remark at the end, that the counterexample "does not use any property of the birhizomic topology", is a sign the author should have heeded.

**Correctness: -1**
**Exposition: 1**
**Attribution: 1**
