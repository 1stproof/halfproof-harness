# Referee report, problem p03 (reduced stated skein algebra of the punctured bigon vs. the generalized quantum double of the simply-connected Borels)

The problem asks for (1) an explicit algebra isomorphism between the reduced stated skein algebra of the once-punctured bigon and the double cross product of the two simply-connected quantum Borel subalgebras with respect to the Drinfeld–Tanisaki skew pairing, and (2) the deduction that the quotient by the ideal generated by the loops `b_ii c_ii - 1` is the simply-connected quantum group. The substantive content (author's solution) is: the saturated-arc splitting of the punctured bigon realises the skein product as the double cross product of two copies of the quantized coordinate algebra via the R-form; reduction at the two ideal vertices cuts each copy down to a triangular quotient; Gavarini's isomorphisms identify the triangular quotients with the Borels; the R-form transported through Gavarini's maps is the Drinfeld–Tanisaki pairing; and the quotient by `G_i^> - G_i^<` is the quantum group.

Line numbers below refer to the `.tex` files in this directory.

## Solution X

**Review.** X does not work with the skein algebra at all. Definition 5 (lines 216–234) *defines* the symbol in the problem as an abstract FRT-type algebra: two triangular matrices `B`, `C` with determinant constraints and three `RLL` relations against the vector-representation R-matrix. No argument is given that this presented algebra is the reduced stated skein algebra of the punctured bigon (the Remark at lines 236–238 merely asserts that the `RLL` relations "encode the local skein relations"). That identification is the core of part (1), so the problem has been replaced by an easier, standard-looking one. Within the replaced problem nothing is proved either: Proposition 7 (lines 252–278), the existence of triangular L-operators in the generalized double satisfying `RLL`, is cited to "the standard FRT construction" in Klimyk–Schmüdgen and Chari–Pressley, which treat the FRT presentation of `U_q(gl_n)`/`U_q(sl_n)` itself, not of the double of simply-connected Borels with two independent tori; surjectivity in Theorem 8 (line 300) rests on the unproved claim that the entries of `L^±` generate the embedded Borels; injectivity (lines 302–310) is "the classical theorem identifying the FRT presentation with the Drinfeld double presentation", with inverse formulas for `E_i`, `F_i` written down without verification (no sign is present although the pairing value `1/(q^{-1}-q)` forces one in the author's normalisation; the formulas are never checked against any relation). Part (2), Proposition 10 (lines 332–354), says "one checks that these elements satisfy exactly the defining relations" and gives no computation; the one concrete fact used, `Φ(b_ii)=ι_-(K_i)`, `Φ(c_ii)=ι_+(K_i)`, is an item of the unproved Proposition 7. The paper therefore consists of definitions, correct general statements of what should be true, and references in place of every proof. What is claimed: the full problem for all `n`. What is established: nothing beyond the (easy) observation that the ideal `I` maps to the Cartan-identification ideal, conditional on Proposition 7.

## Solution Y

**Review.** Y sets up the simply-connected quantum group with its own coproduct convention (Definition 1.1, lines 110–145, consistency correctly checked in Remark 1.2), a skew pairing of the two Borels with the problem's values, and verifies by hand that the pairing is compatible with the Cartan relations of both Borels (Lemma 1.6; I re-derived (i) and (ii) and they are right). The pairing table (Lemma 1.5) is correct. The double is then defined by the kernel (SK) at line 239 with the antipode *inverse* in the first Sweedler slot. In the Klimyk–Schmüdgen/Majid convention that Y's pairing axioms (PA) actually match, the standard kernel has `S`, not `S^{-1}`, in that slot; Y admits (Remark 1.3, lines 167–183, and lines 258–269) that the `S^{-1}` kernel was *chosen because* it reproduces the relations Y wants, and attributes existence/associativity of this non-standard double to Drinfeld–Tanisaki, which cover only the standard one. The central computation, Proposition 1.8 (the cross commutator, lines 587–669), is wrong as written: the first surviving channel uses "`<F_s, E_r K_r^{-1}> = δθ` from Lemma 1.5(d)", but Lemma 1.5(d) (line 322) gives `δθq^{-2}` for that entry (I confirm `q^{-2}`: it is `θ<K^{-1},K^{-1}> = θq^{α·α}`). With the correct value the kernel (SK) yields `[E,F] = θ(K^{(+)} - q^{-2}K^{(-)-1})`, which does not specialise to the quantum-group relation. Y then "cancels the spurious factor `q^{-2}`" by multiplying the displayed relation (EF) by a unit and appealing to "exact normal-form reduction" (lines 653–668); this cannot turn (EF), `[E,F] = θ(K^{(+)} - K^{(-)-1})`, into the stated (EF'), `[E,F] = θ(K^{(+)-1} - K^{(-)})`, which has both tori inverted and the opposite sign in the diagonal quotient. So Proposition 1.8, and with it the mixed relation (M2) in Theorem 2.4(e) and the identification of the quotient in Lemma 3.3, are not established. Further sign slips: the third formula of (H1±) at line 828 contradicts (H1) (the internal `F`-conjugation has exponent `-α_r(i)`), and the `c`-side chains at lines 863–877 are arithmetically inconsistent with their own conclusions (e.g. `q^{+1}F K^{-1}K^{-1}` is followed by `= q^{-1} s s`), one of which also contradicts Definition 2.1's `q^{-1}`. On the skein side, Definition 2.1 (lines 683–800) is a "declared presentation" with entries at gap ≥2 *defined* by a two-term recursion (q-min) that is not the quantum-matrix relation, and mixed relations (M0)–(M3) reverse-engineered from the double; the identification with the actual stated skein algebra is attributed to Lê's triangular decomposition paper, which contains no such presentation of a punctured bigon. Injectivity in Theorem 2.6 (lines 1181–1206) invokes a "PBW theorem for the triangular extended (Lauve–Taft) matrix algebra" and a triangularity claim, neither proved. Part 2 (Section 3) is structurally correct (loops central, ideal equals the torus-identification ideal) but rests on Theorem 2.6 and Proposition 1.8. The repeated appeals to unseen "machine verification" (Remark 2.5) do not substitute for the missing or wrong derivations and are contradicted by the Lemma 1.5(d) vs. Proposition 1.8 discrepancy. What is claimed: the full problem for all `n`. What is established: the Hopf-algebra set-up, the pairing table, the torus cross-relations (Lemma 1.7), and the shape of the argument for part (2); the cross commutator, the skein identification and injectivity are not.

## Solution Z

**Review.** Z explicitly restricts to `n = 2` (abstract, Section 1, Section 7) and solves that case by translation into Korinman's work. Proposition 3.1 reduces Korinman's presentation of the unreduced punctured-bigon algebra (quoted as Corollary 2.15 of arXiv:1907.01732) by the two bad arcs, correctly derives two-sided invertibility of the diagonal arcs from the determinant and diagonal-commutator relations, and lists the six reduced relations; the reduction itself is argued, the unreduced input is cited. Proposition 4.2 presents the rank-two double `D_qB` as in Korinman's Definition 2.18 and asserts without computation that it is the double cross product for the problem's pairing (lines 370–405: "substituting the pairing values into the cross relations gives..."); I checked one torus–root cross relation against the Klimyk–Schmüdgen double with the problem's `q^{1/2 - δ}` torus pairing and it agrees, so the claim is right but unproved in the text. Lemma 5.1 verifies every defining relation of `D_qB` under the explicit arc assignment (lines 623–633); all computations are correct (I rechecked the four twists and the commutator, which lands on `(q-q^{-1})(x^2-y^2)`). Proposition 4.1 (grouplikes of the two halves commute) and Proposition 4.3 (torus-centrality criterion for general `n`) are correct but the latter is not connected to the skein side. Section 7 lists what is missing for general `n`. What is claimed: exactly the `n = 2` case, with injectivity imported. What is established: that, modulo Korinman's theorem, with correct and checkable computations.
