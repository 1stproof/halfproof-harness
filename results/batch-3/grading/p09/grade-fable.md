# Referee report, Problem 9 (non-varying factor of the boundary of a deformed totally geodesic surface)

Reference point. The author's solution works as follows: the projection of $\partial V_0$ to a varying factor is totally geodesic (Benirschke–Dozier–Rached, Arana-Herrera–Wright) hence non-compact, so one degenerates further into a deeper stratum $\Delta_{\Lambda\cup\Lambda'}$; by Arana-Herrera–Wright the nodes of $\Lambda$ can be unpinched near that point, so the deeper boundary piece is locally the frontier of $\overline V_0\cap\Delta_{\Lambda'}$; hypothesis (ii) transfers this to all $t$; the projection of $\overline V_0\cap\Delta_{\Lambda'}$ to the undegenerated factor $\M_{c'}$ is a one-dimensional totally geodesic subvariety, i.e. a Teichmüller curve, rigid under holomorphic deformation by McMullen (2009); its boundary point in $\M_c$ is therefore $t$-independent, and by (ii) that boundary point is $Y_t$. The two essential inputs are (a) the boundary/unpinching theorems for totally geodesic subvarieties and (b) McMullen's rigidity of Teichmüller curves. None of the three submissions identifies input (b), and only Z gropes toward input (a).

## Solution X

X does not claim to solve the problem; its abstract and conclusion state that "the unconditional statement in the original problem remains open." What it proves: (1) the conclusion is equivalent to constancy of the descended map $t\mapsto Y_t$, which is a priori smooth (Prop. 3.1; correct, though Z shows the map is in fact holomorphic by Remmert, so X's caution is unnecessary); (2) the pushforward of a compact boundary curve's fundamental class under $\pi_i$ vanishes (Lemma 4.1; true and vacuous, since the class of a curve mapped to a point is trivially zero); (3) conditional "pinning" theorems (Thms 5.6, 6.3): if $\overline V_0$ admits a no-excess frontier chain from $\Delta_\Gamma$ down to a zero-dimensional stratum, or to a one-dimensional stratum a whole component of which lies in $\overline V_0$, then $Y_t=Y_0$. The proofs are correct but the mechanism is nearly tautological (a smoothly trivial family of points in a discrete set is constant, Lemma 5.2), and X never attempts to show that total geodesy produces such a chain, which is where the whole difficulty lies. (4) A local model in $\mathbb C^4$ with $Y_t=t$ (Prop. 7.1), showing the stratified hypotheses alone do not suffice. This is correct but beside the point, since it discards total geodesy of $V_0$, the hypothesis the problem turns on; X says so itself (Cor. 7.2). (5) Prop. 8.1 correctly records, via Wright and Benirschke–Dozier–Rached, that $V_0$ is a closed algebraic totally geodesic subvariety and that the components of $\partial V_0$ are totally geodesic in $\Delta_\Gamma$; Prop. 8.2 then observes that these theorems do not apply to $V_t$, $t\neq0$, and stops. The missing idea is that the one-dimensional totally geodesic pieces produced at $t=0$ are Teichmüller curves, and that Teichmüller curves are rigid under holomorphic deformation, which is exactly the propagation mechanism X says it cannot find. No false mathematical statements were found; the document is an honest, well-organized, but essentially contentless partial result. Attribution: Wright, Deligne–Mumford and Knudsen are cited correctly; the boundary theorem is cited to "N. Benirschke, E. Dozier, S. Riedel, Totally geodesic submanifolds of moduli spaces of curves, arXiv:2412.06765" — the arXiv number is right but the authors' initials, the third author's name (Rached, not Riedel) and the title are wrong.

## Solution Y

Y asserts that the statement to be proved is false and offers a counterexample (Section 2). The construction plumbs $\{a(t)\}\times C\times\mathbb D^*$ into $\M_g$ near a separating boundary divisor, with $a:\mathbb D\to\M_{g_1,1}$ non-constant, so that $\partial V_t=\{a(t)\}\times C$ and $\pi_1(\partial V_t)=\{a(t)\}$ moves. The construction is fine as a local analytic family, but it ignores the hypothesis that $V_0$ is totally geodesic for the Teichmüller metric: Y's own restatement of the problem in Section 1 reads "Let $V_0\subset\M_g$ be a smooth complex surface" with total geodesy omitted, and the surface $V_0=\mathrm{pl}(\{a(0)\}\times C\times\mathbb D^*)$ is a small plumbing patch which is neither closed in $\M_g$ nor totally geodesic. So Proposition 2.2 does not contradict the problem; it only shows (as X's Section 7 also does) that the stratified hypotheses (i)–(ii) without total geodesy are insufficient, which nobody disputed. The headline claim "the claim is false under the stated hypotheses" is therefore wrong. Section 3 (Theorem 3.1) assumes in addition that every $V_t$ is totally geodesic and deduces $V_t=V_0$ from Wright's finiteness theorem for totally geodesic subvarieties of dimension $>1$; this is correct but, as Y acknowledges, is not the problem (the problem explicitly does not assume $V_t$ totally geodesic for $t\ne0$). Nothing in Y bears on the actual question, and its central assertion is a false statement about the problem. The writing is clear and short. The three references (Deligne–Mumford, Harris–Morrison, Wright) are real and used appropriately.

## Solution Z

Z presents a long argument in the language of Teichmüller dynamics (strata of quadratic differentials, affine invariant manifolds, boundary theory of orbit closures) and states plainly (Thm 1.1, Section 3.3, Appendix B) that the proof is complete only modulo an unproved "Input (Mem)": that the limiting stable differentials of the deformed family $V_t$ stay in the affine invariant manifold $B_0$ generated by the boundary of $\Omega V_0$. Z admits "we cannot honestly complete the proof of the claim from the stated tools alone." The architecture is: constant collapse combinatorics from (ii) (Lemma 3.1), holomorphicity of $t\mapsto Y_t$ via Remmert (Lemma 3.2; conclusion plausible, but the claim that a smoothly trivial family of analytic curves has analytic total space "by Bishop and Rado" is unjustified — the analyticity should instead come from the closure of the complex submanifold $\mathcal V$), constancy of residues (Prop. 3.6), regeneration of a two-parameter holomorphic family into $V_0$ with constant residues (Prop. 2.7, used as a black box), and a contradiction because the regenerated boundary disc lies in $\overline{C_0}$ whose $\pi_i$-image is the single point $Y_0$ (Prop. 4.3). Stripped of the differential-geometric packaging, this is the author's idea that the deeper boundary piece is the frontier of a piece of $\overline V_0$ and that one can "unpinch" into $V_0$, but none of the load-bearing steps is established. Specific defects: (a) Theorem 2.3(2), attributed to Mukamel, asserts that $\M_g$ for $g\ge3$ contains no totally geodesic subvariety of dimension $\ge2$ and that the totally geodesic surfaces in $\M_2$ are McMullen's Hilbert modular surfaces. Both claims are false (McMullen–Mukamel–Wright and Eskin–McMullen–Mukamel–Wright construct totally geodesic surfaces, and the eigenform loci in $\M_2$ are not totally geodesic for the Teichmüller metric); the first would make the problem's own hypothesis vacuous for $g\ge3$, an internal contradiction Z does not notice. Lemma 2.4 builds on this. (b) Section 2.1 states $\dim\Omega V=2\dim V-1$; for a totally geodesic surface the set of generating differentials is a $\mathbb C^*$-bundle over a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle over $V$, of dimension 4, not 3, so the dimension bookkeeping in Remarks 2.2 and Lemma 4.2 Step 2 is off. (c) Prop. 3.6 argues that ratios of moduli of collapsing cylinders are "continuous $\mathbb Q_{>0}$-valued functions, hence constant"; cylinder modulus ratios are real numbers with no rationality, so the argument is invalid. (d) Lemma 4.2 Step 1 claims $W_0=\overline V_0\cap\Delta_\Gamma^\circ$ satisfies $W_0\setminus\Delta_\Gamma=\varnothing$, but $\Delta_\Gamma^\circ$ as defined contains $\M_g$ itself, so $W_0\supset V_0$ and the step is false; the inclusion it is meant to prove (the deeper boundary of $\overline V_0$ lies in $\overline{C_0}$) is the genuine content of the Arana-Herrera–Wright unpinching result and is not proved here. (e) Prop. 2.7 (regeneration) is cited to "Mazor–Wright, Theorem 7.4 and §8", a reference that does not exist (the boundary theory of affine invariant manifolds is Mirzakhani–Wright). The relevant literature on boundaries of totally geodesic subvarieties and the rigidity of Teichmüller curves is not mentioned. Exposition is dense, jargon-heavy, and contains editorial residue ("corrects a previous version", "per the plan's honesty clause"); it can be followed with effort. Attribution: Eskin–Mirzakhani, Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi, Filip, McMullen 2007, Smillie–Weiss and BCGGM are real and roughly correctly described, but the "Mukamel" theorem and its paper are fabricated (Z itself flags the reference as unverifiable), "Mazor–Wright" with specific theorem numbers is fabricated, and the Nielsen realization theorem (Kerckhoff) is misattributed as the source of finite level covers.
