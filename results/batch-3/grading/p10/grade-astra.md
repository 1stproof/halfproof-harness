# Referee reports

These reports use only the five supplied TeX files. References to external literature are assessed from their use in the submissions and the supplied author solution; no external sources were consulted. Line numbers refer to the submitted source files.

## X

X correctly identifies the orthogonal complement of the polarising hyperbolic plane, the admissibility parameter 1, and the mirror lattice \(H\oplus E_8^{\oplus2}\) (Section 2). It explicitly acknowledges that it does not construct an allowable loop or establish the mirror-pair conditions. However, it goes beyond an acknowledged partial solution by asserting that an unconditional proof is unavailable because the broader statement appears as a conjecture (abstract, lines 30–35; Sections 3 and 5). This inference is invalid: a conjecture can have provable special cases, and the supplied author solution addresses precisely this special case through the degeneration classification and explicit splittings. The restatement at lines 51–62 also omits the problem's corrected componentwise positivity hypothesis, so the claimed exact identification of statements is inaccurate. There is a further mathematical overstatement in the elliptic-fibration proposition (lines 165–179): an arbitrary prescribed copy of \(H\) need not contain a nef primitive isotropic class. The standard argument uses reflections to make an isotropic class nef and can change the embedded copy of \(H\); it does not establish the asserted fibre class in the original summand. Attributing this stronger assertion to Huybrechts is consequently problematic. The elementary lattice progress is useful and clearly presented, but the erroneous status conclusion and strengthened fibration claim warrant a deduction beyond the rubric's score for a merely acknowledged partial result. No mirror pair is established.

Correctness: -0.5

## Y

Y is a substantial, explicitly acknowledged partial solution. Its strongest contribution is the constructive proof of double admissibility and the orthogonal decompositions in Section 3 (particularly lines 222–304), which correctly allow either rank-16 even unimodular negative-definite complement. The primitive-embedding argument and the conditional monodromy arithmetic are also useful. Section 6 gives a valid elementary obstruction for the listed excluded Euler-number values using subset sums and products of two Dehn twists. Nevertheless, neither a compatible fibration and loop nor the required simultaneous lattice isometry is constructed; Section 7 explicitly admits this. The numerical blow-up examples in Sections 5–6 do not establish occurrence under the corrected polarisation and very-generality hypotheses, as the text itself acknowledges. The positivity discussion (lines 743–815) establishes only that the mirror definitions do not explicitly evaluate the restriction squares: it does not establish that positivity is dispensable in the existence problem. Similarly, exclusion of the balanced case in Section 5 is a self-imposed limitation, not a consequence of Type II monodromy. Calling the generic \(2\mathrm{II}^*+4\mathrm I_1\) fibration “extremal” is incorrect in the usual sense: its Picard number is 18, not 20. The final list of “open” points, especially line 827, should distinguish matters unresolved by this submission from open mathematical questions; the supplied reference already describes the standard and alternate fibrations. These qualifications do not destroy the main partial lattice results, but the central existence theorem remains entirely unproved, so the acknowledged-partial-result score applies. The exposition is lengthy but organized and explicit about most conditional hypotheses. Attributions are essentially appropriate, including the lattice-incidence criterion matching the use of GT Proposition 5.3 in the reference solution; the abbreviated Alexeev–Engel bibliography entry could be improved, and its precise theorem statement cannot be checked from the supplied files.

Correctness: 0

## Z

Z claims a complete construction, but its central argument is unsalvageable. The lattice duality calculation and displayed generic Weierstrass equation are useful preliminary facts; they do not rescue the subsequent proof. Section 4 (lines 425–524) misidentifies a Tyurin degeneration as Type III: here the degeneration is Type II, with nonzero monodromy logarithm satisfying \(N^2=0\). The dual complex of two components meeting along one connected double curve is an interval, not the asserted sphere. The fibration argument already contains false statements: at lines 268–270 it says that all roots of \(H\oplus E_8^{\oplus2}\) lie in the two \(E_8\)'s, although its own class \(\sigma\) has square \(-2\); Section 3.3 infers uniqueness from a vanishing Mordell–Weil rank, overlooking the alternate fibration described in the reference. The contraction discussion (lines 377–418) wrongly includes every component of the \(\mathrm{II}^*\) fibres among the curves orthogonal to \(h\): the component meeting the section has \(h\)-degree 1. The allowability proof (lines 553–583) substitutes invented Type III conditions for the required boundary-monodromy and primitivity conditions. Its base-change assertions are also false: a cover unramified at an \(\mathrm I_1\) point does not change its local fibre into \(\mathrm I_6\), and \(\mathrm I_0^*\) does not have trivial monodromy. The lattice comparison is internally impossible (lines 628–720): a rank-18 group modulo a rank-one subgroup cannot still have rank 18; a rank-20 group modulo that subgroup cannot have rank 20; and the form on \(N\) does not descend to \(N/\mathbb Zf\), since \(f\cdot\sigma=1\). The text also changes the specified pairings \(f'\cdot g=0\) and \(f\cdot g=1\) when computing the purported quotient form. Finally, the alleged GT definitions and “Theorem C” (lines 723–769) introduce checkerboards, sphere dual complexes, and filtration conditions incompatible with the actual lattice-incidence framework recorded in the supplied reference. These are substantive hallucinated attributions, alongside the misattribution of indefinite lattice classification to Niemeier. Neither allowability, compatibility, nor mirror matching is established. Although the section structure is readable, contradictory notation and impossible constructions make the argument difficult to follow as mathematics.

Correctness: -1

Attribution: -1
