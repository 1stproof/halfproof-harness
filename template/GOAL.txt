/goal Outcome: settle the research-level mathematics question in PROBLEM.md and write the answer, with a complete rigorous argument, to FINAL.tex (a compilable LaTeX document meeting the standards of a research journal).

How to work (you have many hours and a large budget; use them):
0. MEMORY. You work in a series of sessions. Each session starts with an empty memory and ends without warning; the files in this directory are the only thing that carries over. Therefore:
   - At the start of every session, read NOTES.md, EXPERIMENTS.md and AUDIT.md (if present) before doing anything else, and continue from the "Next step" recorded there.
   - After EVERY experiment or piece of reasoning that produced a result (positive, negative, or a dead end), append it to EXPERIMENTS.md or NOTES.md immediately, before starting the next thing: what you tried, the exact command/script in code/, the outcome, and what it implies. Never let more than a few tool calls pass without writing.
   - Keep at the top of NOTES.md a short "Current state" section: what is established (with where it is justified), current best guess for the answer and why, open questions, and "Next step". Rewrite it whenever it changes.
   Work that is not written down is lost and will be redone.
1. Understand before attacking. Restate every definition in NOTES.md in your own words and work out the smallest nontrivial instances completely by hand and by computer.
2. Do not assume which way the answer goes. If the question asks whether something holds, treat "yes" and "no" as equally live until you have evidence. The wording of a question is not evidence. A proof of the wrong answer is worth nothing.
3. Experiment. You have a shell with Python (write your own code; install nothing and use no network). Encode the definitions exactly as stated, enumerate or compute systematically across many small and medium cases, covering the full generality of every parameter in the statement rather than only the simplest special case, and let the data suggest the answer and the mechanism. Record every experiment and its outcome in EXPERIMENTS.md. Double-check that your code implements the definitions as literally written; an implementation shortcut that silently changes the definition invalidates the data.
4. Pursue several genuinely different approaches. When one stalls, write down precisely where and why in NOTES.md, and switch. Revisit the question of which answer is true whenever an approach fails in a suspicious way.
5. Referee yourself. Once you have a candidate argument, attack every step as a hostile referee would: look for unproved claims, special cases, hidden assumptions, and statements that "clearly" hold. Test each nontrivial lemma computationally where possible. Log the audit in AUDIT.md and fix every gap; if a gap cannot be fixed, the argument is not finished.
6. If the answer is negative, the final document must exhibit an explicit, fully verified counterexample (with the verification written out so a reader can check it by hand). If positive, a complete proof. Either way: careful lemma and theorem statements, complete proofs or precise citations of standard published results for every intermediate claim.

Done when: FINAL.tex states the answer in its first paragraph, contains the full argument with no placeholders, gaps, "sketch", or "it can be shown"; AUDIT.md records a final line-by-line audit of FINAL.tex that found no unresolved issues; and the command `test -s FINAL.tex && grep -q 'begin{document}' FINAL.tex && echo FINAL_PRESENT` prints FINAL_PRESENT.

Must not: use the web or any network resource, modify PROBLEM.md, present numerical evidence as proof, or stop at an outline. Keep NOTES.md current so work survives context compaction. On block: write BLOCKED.md with what is proved, the exact remaining gap, the approaches tried, and the best next step, then continue working on that next step.
